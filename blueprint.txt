MINESIGHT — SYSTEM BLUEPRINT
AI-Based Smart Governance & Compliance Monitoring System for Coal Mines
Smart India Hackathon 2026 — Software Track
2-Role MVP: Contractor + Supervisor
================================================================================


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 0 — THE PROBLEM, IN FULL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Indian coal mining operations involve mine management, multiple independent
contractors, contract labour, safety inspection duties, and environmental
monitoring, all running in parallel across one site. Today, most of the
governance work behind this — statutory compliance, safety observations,
license/certificate tracking, worker attendance, environmental readings — is
handled through paper registers, spreadsheets, and email.

This causes five concrete failures:
  1. Data inconsistency — the same fact (e.g. a contractor's license status)
     exists in multiple places and can disagree with itself.
  2. Delayed decision-making — a supervisor may not learn about a problem
     until days after it happened.
  3. Limited transparency — management cannot see real-time site conditions.
  4. Compliance gaps — expired licenses or overdue inspections go unnoticed
     until an audit or incident forces the issue.
  5. Weak field-level monitoring — verbal warnings are given but never
     formally tracked to resolution.

MineSight's job is to replace the fragmented paper/spreadsheet layer with one
connected system, without pretending to solve problems outside its scope
(it does not replace real IoT hardware, it does not automate legal judgment,
and it does not manage production/finance).


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 1 — WHY ONLY TWO ROLES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Earlier drafts of this platform considered up to seven roles (Mine Manager,
Compliance Officer, Field Inspector, Supervisor, Contractor, Regional
Management, Admin). For a student team building a working hackathon
prototype, that scope was not realistic — a fully working 2-role system beats
a half-built 7-role system in judging every time.

MineSight is deliberately reduced to:
  - CONTRACTOR — the party responsible for one specific work package
    (e.g. blasting, transportation, excavation/overburden removal,
    maintenance), including their own workers, machinery, and documents.
  - SUPERVISOR — the party overseeing all contractors and all task types
    on a site, responsible for field observations, environmental/yield
    tracking, and verifying that flagged issues are actually fixed.

There is no separate "Field Inspector" role — that function is merged into
Supervisor. There is no separate "Compliance Officer" or "Mine Manager" role
in this MVP — the Supervisor's dashboard and the Platform-Wide Warning
System together cover the oversight function those roles would have
performed, without the added build cost of a third portal.

Everything in this document is scoped to what these two roles need.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 2 — CONTRACTOR PORTAL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Contractor Portal is designed to be simple and mobile-friendly, because
the people using it on the ground are often busy, on-site, and not sitting
at a desk. Every module below is something the Contractor's own team
(Project Manager, Site Supervisor, Accountant, Store/Logistics — the
Contractor's internal staff) maintains for their own work package.

2.1  Digital License & Certificate Wallet
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
What it does:
  Contractors upload their labour licenses, equipment fitness papers, and
  worker insurance documents. The system extracts and tracks each
  document's expiry date automatically, and flags anything expired or
  nearing expiry.

Why it exists:
  Certificate expiry is one of the most common real compliance failures in
  contracted operations — not because the certificate was never obtained,
  but because nobody was tracking the date. A spreadsheet cell that says
  "expires 12 Sep" is easy to miss; a system that turns red on 12 Sep is not.

What it actually tracks (grounded in real Indian mining-contractor
requirements, not invented):
  - Contract Labour License — mandatory once a contractor employs 20 or
    more workers on any day, issued by the state/central Labour
    Department under the Contract Labour (Regulation & Abolition) Act.
  - Blaster's / Shot-firer's Certificate (DGMS) — held by a named
    individual, required for anyone conducting blasting. Only relevant
    for contractors whose task_type is "blasting."
  - Explosives License — separate from the Blaster's Certificate,
    required for storing/handling explosives. Only relevant for
    "blasting" task_type.
  - Equipment fitness / approval certificates — DGMS approval and/or BIS
    license required for many categories of mining machinery before they
    can legally be used.
  - Worker insurance documents — proof of coverage for the contractor's
    labour force.

Each document has: document type, holder (person or equipment ID), a
document/certificate number, an expiry date, and a status
(Valid / Expiring soon / Expired). Expired or expiring documents are what
feed the Supervisor's Contractor Status Dashboard and the red-flag system
described in Section 3.

2.2  Digital Attendance Register
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
What it does:
  Replaces the physical paper attendance register with a quick daily
  headcount entry: total workers, present, absent, on leave.

Why it exists:
  Attendance is one of the simplest and most frequently-needed pieces of
  operational data, and it is currently one of the most commonly faked or
  delayed pieces of paperwork (a register filled in after the fact, not
  during the shift). A same-day digital entry closes that gap without
  requiring biometric hardware, which is out of scope for a student
  prototype.

What it is NOT:
  It is not a proof of safety compliance on its own — it is one signal
  among several. It does not replace the more detailed Worker Roster
  (below) which tracks training and PPE status per worker.

2.3  Worker Roster (per-worker detail, distinct from attendance)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
What it does:
  A record for each worker under the contractor, separate from the
  Team Roster of management staff. Tracks: worker ID/name, role
  (helper, machine operator, etc.), training status
  (Complete / Missing), and PPE issued status (Yes / Pending).

Why it exists:
  Attendance tells you someone showed up. It does not tell you whether
  that person is trained or equipped to be there safely. This roster is
  the missing link between attendance data and the Supervisor's safety
  observations — if a Supervisor logs a PPE violation against a specific
  worker, this roster shows whether that worker's training was already a
  known gap, which makes the eventual warning/audit trail far more
  informative than a bare "PPE issue found."

Who maintains it:
  The Contractor's own staff (Project Manager / Site Supervisor role
  within the contractor's team) — not the platform's Supervisor role.
  This keeps the RBAC boundary clean: the Supervisor can view this data
  during a site walk but does not edit it.

2.4  Machinery Register (with maintenance tracking)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
What it does:
  Lists every machine assigned to this contractor's work package:
  machine type, quantity, ownership status (owned / financed / rented),
  last serviced date, next maintenance due date, and current status
  (Active / Due soon / Overdue / Under maintenance). Each machine is also
  linked to its fitness/approval certificate from the License Wallet.

Why it exists:
  Machinery in active use (hydraulic excavators, dumpers, drilling
  machines, water tankers, graders, dozers) requires periodic maintenance
  and a valid approval certificate to be legally and safely operated. A
  machine running past its "next due" maintenance date with no record of
  service is a real operational risk, not a cosmetic detail — and it is
  exactly the kind of thing a fragmented paper system loses track of.

Which machines apply to which contractor:
  This is where task_type (Section 4) matters — a blasting contractor's
  machinery list (excavators, drilling machines) is different from a
  transport contractor's (dumpers, water tankers). The register only
  shows the machines relevant to that contractor's actual work.

2.5  Explosives Stock Register (blasting contractors only)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
What it does:
  Tracks explosives procured, used, and remaining balance, by type
  (e.g. site-mix emulsion, detonators).

Why it exists:
  Unaccounted-for explosives is a genuinely serious real-world compliance
  and safety issue in blasting operations — this is not a feature added
  for novelty points, it is one of the most operationally meaningful
  things this platform tracks. It only appears for contractors whose
  task_type is "blasting"; it is irrelevant and hidden for all other
  contractor types.

2.6  Unresolved Action Center
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
What it does:
  A read-only feed, visible on the Contractor's home screen, showing any
  active safety remarks or warnings the Supervisor has logged against
  them. Displayed in clear, bold red text specifically so it is
  impossible to miss — including for a busy or non-technical contractor
  who may not read every notification carefully.

Why it exists:
  This is the other half of the Observation Logger (Section 3.2). A
  warning that only the Supervisor can see is not useful — the point of
  the loop is that the Contractor is immediately and unmissably aware
  something needs fixing, without needing to be told separately by phone
  or in person.

What the Contractor can do from here:
  Upload proof of correction (photo, renewed document, etc.) against the
  specific open remark, which sends it to the Supervisor for Closure
  Verification (Section 3.5).


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 3 — SUPERVISOR PORTAL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Supervisor oversees every contractor and every task type on the site.
This role absorbs what would otherwise be a separate "Field Inspector"
role — one person visits sites, logs observations, and later verifies that
issues are actually resolved, rather than splitting that into two handoffs.

3.1  Contractor Status Dashboard
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
What it does:
  A list of every active contractor under the Supervisor's zone, with
  invalid/expiring certifications and open penalties surfaced at a
  glance — not buried inside each contractor's individual profile.

Why it exists:
  Without this, a Supervisor would need to open every contractor's
  profile individually to notice a problem. This view exists specifically
  so risk is visible without hunting for it — the same principle behind
  every "explain why" style feature elsewhere in the platform: don't make
  the user go looking for bad news.

3.2  Quick Observation Logger ("Report Issue")
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
What it does:
  The Supervisor walks the site, selects a category (PPE, Blasting, Dust,
  Equipment, or Other), attaches a photo, writes a short remark, and tags
  a severity level (Low / Medium / High / Critical). Submitting this
  immediately locks an unresolved warning onto the relevant contractor's
  profile.

Why it exists:
  This is the single most important data-entry action in the whole
  system — it is the moment a real-world problem becomes a tracked,
  timestamped, evidenced record instead of a verbal warning that may or
  may not be remembered or acted on.

What happens automatically on submit:
  - An Observation ID is generated.
  - The relevant contractor's profile is red-flagged (visible instantly
    in their Unresolved Action Center).
  - The entry is timestamped and photo-evidenced for the eventual audit
    trail.
  - Status is set to OPEN until Closure Verification (3.5) clears it.

3.3  Daily Yield & Environment Tracker
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
What it does:
  Logs daily coal tonnage collected, and records environmental readings —
  air pollution/dust levels, waste water/effluent levels — against
  defined acceptable limits.

Why it exists:
  Production output (yield) and environmental compliance are normally
  tracked in entirely separate systems, if they are tracked digitally at
  all. Putting them in the same daily log as the Supervisor's other
  duties means environmental monitoring doesn't require separate
  hardware or a separate workflow — it is simply another data point
  logged on the same visit.

Important honesty note carried over from earlier design work:
  For the student prototype, environmental readings are entered manually
  or from clearly-labelled simulated data — this platform does not claim
  to have deployed real IoT sensors. What matters for the MVP is that the
  reading, once entered (by any means), is compared against a defined
  threshold and acted on consistently — not that the reading itself came
  from real hardware.

3.4  Platform-Wide Warning System
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
What it does:
  If an environmental reading or safety threshold is exceeded anywhere in
  the system, a warning flag triggers instantly and is visible across the
  dashboard — not just on the specific contractor's profile where it
  originated.

Why it exists:
  This is what replaces the "explainable risk score" concept from the
  earlier 3-role design (which had a dedicated Compliance Officer role).
  Without a separate compliance/risk-scoring role, the Supervisor still
  needs a way to see "something is wrong somewhere" without having to
  check every contractor and every reading manually. This feature is
  the load-bearing replacement for that missing role — deterministic,
  rule-based (threshold exceeded → flag), never a black-box AI number.

3.5  Closure Verification
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
What it does:
  Once a Contractor uploads proof that a flagged issue has been resolved
  (a corrected photo, a renewed certificate, etc.), the Supervisor reviews
  it and clicks Verify & Approve. This clears the red warning from the
  Contractor's profile and writes a permanent entry to the audit trail.

Why it exists:
  This is what actually closes the loop — the difference between a
  system that just collects reports and one that proves problems get
  fixed. A permanent audit trail (who raised it, when, what evidence was
  given, who verified it, when) is what makes the platform's governance
  claim credible rather than cosmetic.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 4 — THE task_type FIELD (HOW ONE SYSTEM SERVES DIFFERENT CONTRACTORS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Different contractors do fundamentally different work — a blasting
contractor's needs (explosives stock, blaster's certificate) are not the
same as a transportation contractor's (dumper fleet, trip logs) or an
excavation contractor's (overburden volume tracking).

Rather than building separate portals or separate logins per contractor
type, MineSight uses a single field on the Contractor's profile:

    task_type: blasting | transportation | excavation | maintenance

This field is set ONCE, at the time the contractor is onboarded into the
system (i.e. when their contract/tender is awarded and a Supervisor or
admin creates their profile) — it is not something the contractor is
asked to choose every time they log in, because it is a fixed property
of the contract itself, not a session preference.

From then on:
  - The Contractor simply logs in and sees the sections relevant to their
    task_type automatically (e.g. Explosives Stock Register only appears
    for "blasting").
  - The Machinery Register filters to show only the machine types
    relevant to that task.
  - The Daily Work Log (if built into the Contractor Portal) shows the
    right output metric for the job — blasting rounds fired, tonnage
    moved, or cubic meters of overburden removed.
  - Core sections that apply to every contractor regardless of task type
    (Team Roster, License Wallet, Attendance, Worker Roster, Unresolved
    Action Center) remain visible for all.

Edge case to decide before building, not after:
  If a single contractor genuinely performs more than one task type under
  one contract, task_type can be made a multi-select field instead of a
  single value, so the profile shows the union of relevant sections. This
  is a minor schema decision now versus a larger rework later if left
  until the build is underway.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 5 — END-TO-END DEMO WORKFLOW (2-ROLE VERSION)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This is the single story to run live at the internal round demo.

  1. THE SETUP
     A contractor's profile shows either a clean status or a license
     expiring soon, visible in their License & Certificate Wallet.

  2. THE FIELD CHECK
     The Supervisor visits a mining pit or blasting site, spots a safety
     violation (e.g. workers without required PPE, or dust levels beyond
     the acceptable limit), and logs an observation with a photo and
     severity tag through the Observation Logger.

  3. THE WARNING TRIGGER
     The system instantly flags an open issue on the contractor's
     profile, shown in bold red text in their Unresolved Action Center.
     If the issue is environmental and crosses a defined threshold, the
     Platform-Wide Warning System also fires.

  4. THE RESOLUTION LOOP
     The Contractor uploads corrected work proof or a renewed document.
     The Supervisor reviews it, clicks "Verify & Approve." The red
     warning disappears, and the action is permanently sealed into the
     audit trail.

Why this is the right demo scenario: it requires nothing fabricated (no
fake hardware claims), it touches both roles, and it shows the platform's
actual differentiator — a warning that is raised, tracked, and provably
resolved, not just logged and forgotten.

SECTION 6 — AI SAFETY & DATA PRIVACY RULES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

These rules govern any AI or automated component built into MineSight —
document parsers, OCR extraction, or any rule engine driving warnings.

6.1  Zero Data Leakage & Privacy Compliance
  - No AI model or OCR document extractor processing contractor licenses,
    worker counts, or safety records may leak personally identifiable
    information (PII) or sensitive operational data to external logging
    systems.
  - Any uploaded certificate or document must be processed securely —
    raw files are scrubbed of unauthorized credentials before being
    indexed or stored.

6.2  Deterministic AI — No Hallucinated Compliance
  - Any warning, flag, or "risk" signal the system produces must be
    driven solely by deterministic rules applied to real data inputs
    (e.g. an actual expiry date has passed; an actual observation is
    still open; an actual reading exceeded an actual threshold).
    Black-box AI guessing is not permitted anywhere in this system.
  - If a warning is shown, the system must be able to state exactly which
    data point caused it — never an unexplained flag.
  - The system must never invent, estimate, or hallucinate safety
    inspection data, environmental readings, or attendance numbers. If a
    data point has not been entered, it is shown as "Pending" or "Not
    Logged" — never filled in with a guess.

6.3  Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) Enforcement
  - Contractors may only view and edit their own profile, license status,
    worker roster, and machinery register — never another contractor's
    data.
  - Supervisors may view all contractors in their zone, log observations,
    and verify closures — but may not alter system-wide compliance
    definitions (e.g. what counts as an acceptable dust threshold)
    without separate authorization.

6.4  Guardrails Against Malicious Prompt Injection & Harm
  - Any AI coding assistant operating on this repository is restricted
    from executing unauthorized shell commands, altering environment
    variables, or introducing third-party dependencies with known
    security vulnerabilities.
  - Any AI service (e.g. a document parser) that encounters an unreadable
    file or an internal error must fail gracefully with a descriptive
    error message — never crash the application or expose backend stack
    traces to the user.

SECTION 7 — MVP SCOPE VS. FUTURE SCOPE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

MUST BUILD (MVP):
  - Contractor: License Wallet, Attendance Register, Worker Roster,
    Machinery Register, Unresolved Action Center
  - Supervisor: Contractor Status Dashboard, Observation Logger, Daily
    Yield & Environment Tracker, Platform-Wide Warning System, Closure
    Verification
  - task_type-driven conditional rendering (blasting/transport/
    excavation/maintenance)
  - Full audit trail for every observation → action → verification cycle

SHOULD BUILD IF TIME ALLOWS:
  - Explosives Stock Register (blasting contractors)
  - Document field extraction (auto-reading expiry dates off uploaded
    certificate PDFs)
  - Notification system (push/SMS alerts on new warnings)

EXPLICITLY OUT OF SCOPE FOR THIS BUILD:
  - Real IoT sensor hardware (all environmental data is manual/simulated
    and clearly labelled as such)
  - A separate Compliance Officer, Mine Manager, or Regional/HQ role
  - Grievance management module
  - Production/financial reporting beyond daily tonnage
  - Any AI-generated (non-deterministic) risk scoring


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 8 — WHAT MAKES THIS PLATFORM ACTUALLY DIFFERENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Being direct about this, since "AI + dashboard + compliance" is not
automatically novel — plenty of generic GRC (governance, risk, compliance)
software already does variations of this for other industries.

What MineSight actually claims as its differentiator:
  1. It closes the loop. Most fragmented paper/spreadsheet systems stop
     at "issue noted somewhere." MineSight tracks issue -> red flag ->
     contractor evidence -> supervisor verification -> permanent audit
     trail, end to end, for every single observation.
  2. It adapts to contractor type without splitting into separate
     products. One schema (task_type), not three separate builds.
  3. It connects licenses, machinery, and field observations into one
     place instead of three separate spreadsheets that never talk to
     each other.
  4. It is honest about what is real. No fabricated IoT hardware claims,
     no black-box AI scores — every flag traces back to an actual data
     point a person can check.
